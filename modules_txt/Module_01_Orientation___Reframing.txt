### Why This Might Feel Uncomfortable (And Why That's a Good Sign)

Most coaching systems reward control.
Clear instructions. Clean drills. Predictable outcomes.

This course will ask you to shift where that control lives.

Not because you’re wrong.
But because the game is messier than the methods most of us were taught.

If stepping back feels irresponsible…
If silence feels lazy…
If letting players search feels risky…

You’re not failing.

You’re standing at the edge of real learning.

### Volleyball Is an Open Skill Sport

That’s coach-speak for:
The game refuses to behave.

The ball doesn’t arrive on schedule.
Opponents don’t cooperate.
Teammates make decisions you didn’t script.

And yet…
We often train volleyball like it should behave.
Like if we clean it up enough, it will comply.

Then we act surprised when it falls apart under pressure.

The chaos isn’t the problem.
Our attempt to eliminate it is.

--- 💡 Myths We’re Clearing Up Early ---
“Ecological coaching is chaos.”
> No. It’s intentional instability—designed to produce clarity in action.

“No feedback means no improvement.”
> Wrong. Feedback still matters.
But players adapt faster when they are supported in finding solutions, not just executing instructions.

“This is anti-technique.”
> No. Technique still matters.
But in this approach, form follows function—shaped by constraints, context, and intention, not isolation.

### REFLECTION: QUICK REFLECTION
There are no “right” answers here. You’re just mapping your habits.

Questions/Prompts:
1. During practice, what situations pull you toward intervening the fastest? (Specific moments, not general habits.)
2. In those moments, what are you most tempted to add—information, reassurance, or control?
3. When you choose silence, what do you worry might happen if you don’t step in?
4. After the moment passes, what actually changed—if anything?

Closing Instruction: Don't fix it yet. Just notice it.
