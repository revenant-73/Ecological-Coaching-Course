MODULE 5: Coaching Behavior as a Constraint
Description: Tone, silence, question design. Real coaching language swaps.
=======================================================================================================================

### You Are the Biggest Constraint in the Room

The way you talk, breathe, move, and react shapes player behavior more than any rule change.

If you're anxious → Players are anxious.
If you're commenting on everything → Players wait for your voice.
If you're asking → Players think.

Your behavior is a design element. It must be intentional.

### Tone Regulation

--- TRADITIONAL COACHING VOICE ---
Corrective. Loud. Immediate.

> "No! That's wrong! You need to—"

Effect: Players tune out or become anxious. They stop experimenting.
------------------

--- ECOLOGICAL COACHING VOICE ---
Curious. Calm. Delayed (if at all).

> [Silence while play continues. After rally] "What did you notice about spacing?"

Effect: Players pay attention. They think. They own solutions.
------------------

### Silence as Information

Every time you talk, you prevent a player from thinking.

Long silence feels uncomfortable. That's the point.

Players will fill it. And what they say/do is their real learning—not your interpretation.

--- LANGUAGE SWAP #1: FEEDBACK ---
**INSTEAD OF:**
> "Your footwork is bad. Step into the ball."

**SAY:**
> "How did you decide where to move?"

**Why:** You're shifting from diagnosis to inquiry. The player now owns the observation.
------------------

--- LANGUAGE SWAP #2: ENCOURAGEMENT ---
**INSTEAD OF:**
> "Good job! You're getting better!"

**SAY:**
> "What made that work?"

**Why:** You're making the player conscious of their own strategy, not dependent on your approval.
------------------

--- LANGUAGE SWAP #3: CORRECTION ---
**INSTEAD OF:**
> "You missed the dig because you weren't ready. Stay low."

**SAY:**
> [Modify the game: "Hitter must wait 1 second before attacking." or "Passer must call ball before contact."]

**Why:** You're using design instead of words. Players solve it.
------------------

### REFLECTION: AUDIT YOUR VOICE
Questions/Prompts:
1. During your last practice, when did you talk the most?
2. What were you trying to fix?
3. Could a constraint change have done it instead?

Closing Instruction: Record yourself. Listen. Notice patterns.
