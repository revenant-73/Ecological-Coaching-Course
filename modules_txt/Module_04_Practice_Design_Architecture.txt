MODULE 4: Practice Design Architecture
Description: Game-based framework: Arrival, Main, Transfer, Reflection.
===============================================================================================================

### You Are Not Running Drills. You Are Designing Systems.

Abandon the idea that practice is a sequence of exercises.

Practice is an **architecture**.

It has four phases that work together to:
1. Prepare players emotionally
2. Present the challenge
3. Transfer learning to game complexity
4. Cement understanding

### The Practice Architecture

--- PHASE 1: ARRIVAL GAME (5–10 min) ---
Purpose: Emotional readiness + baseline data

A simple, repeatable game that:
- Requires no instruction
- Reveals individual and team state
- Sets tone (joyful, not militaristic)
- Gives you coaching data before you "teach"

Example: Score to 5 with no scoring rule changes. You just watch.
------------------

--- PHASE 2: MAIN GAME (15–25 min) ---
Purpose: Training the skill you identified

A game with **intentional constraints** that make the desired behavior unavoidable.

Examples:
- "Setter must move with the ball" → Sets up better timing
- "First contact setter must be touching the net" → Forces passers to pass deeper
- "Blocker gets +2 points for block" → Rewards intelligent positioning

You stay mostly silent. Observe patterns.
------------------

--- PHASE 3: TRANSFER GAME (10–15 min) ---
Purpose: Full game complexity

Remove the constraint or modify it less aggressively.

Now the players apply what they discovered under normal pressure. This is where you see if the learning transfers.

Stay silent unless a safety issue emerges.
------------------

--- PHASE 4: REFLECTION (3–5 min) ---
Purpose: Conscious understanding

Not "Here's what you did wrong."

Instead:
> "What did you notice changed when we removed that rule?"

Let players articulate the learning. Then add nothing.
------------------

### Why Fewer Games Matter

Running five different drills means:
- Setup changes (5 minutes)
- Transition confusion (5 minutes)
- Rules repeated five times (5 minutes)
- Shallow learning (no depth in any one thing)

Running one game for 30 minutes with evolving constraints means:
- One setup
- One set of rules (modified, not replaced)
- Natural repetition emergence
- Deep learning in one challenge

### Anchor Games (Sample Set)

Every program needs 3–5 anchor games. These are your defaults:

**Game 1: Wash**
- Full court, normal scoring
- Every game reveals everything
- Constraints applied as needed

**Game 2: King of the Court** (or Rally Scoring)
- Winners stay, losers rotate
- Creates natural intensity and game-speed urgency

**Game 3: 2v2 (or Modified Sides)**
- Isolates decision-making
- Reduces hiding spots
- Forces involvement

--- FOR EACH ANCHOR GAME, DEFINE: ---
**Purpose:** What skill are we developing?
**Reveals:** What does this game expose about player understanding?
**Constraint Sliders:** What can we adjust without changing the game entirely?
**Failure Modes:** How do players cheat or avoid the challenge?
**Language to Avoid:** What instructions would undermine the design?
------------------

### REFLECTION: DESIGN YOUR ANCHOR GAMES
Questions/Prompts:
1. What 3–4 games form the core of your practice?
2. For each: What constraints can you adjust?
3. What would you never say during these games?

Closing Instruction: This is your foundation. Spend time on this.
