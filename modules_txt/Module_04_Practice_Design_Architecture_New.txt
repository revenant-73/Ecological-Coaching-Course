MODULE 4: Practice Design Architecture
Designing sessions that don’t collapse under pressure


From Moments to Systems

By now, you can:

- Notice why you intervene (Module 1)
- See performance as a relationship (Module 2)
- Translate what you see into live constraints (Module 3)

But translation inside a rally is only part of the job.

The bigger question is this:

How do you design an entire practice
so learning compounds instead of resets every 10 minutes?

Practice isn’t a sequence of drills.

It’s an architecture.


Architecture, Not Formula

The four phases below are not a rigid script.

They’re a functional lens.

You may blend them.
You may shorten them.
You may adjust based on age or time.

The goal isn’t perfection.

The goal is depth.


Phase 1: Arrival Game (5–10 Minutes)

Purpose: Emotional readiness + information gathering.

This is not anti-warmup.

Players still move.
Still touch the ball.
Still elevate heart rate.

The difference is this:

You begin inside a representative game.

Simple.
Repeatable.
Low explanation.

Example:
Play to 5. No special rules. You observe.

You’re collecting data:
Energy.
Communication.
Decision tempo.
Movement quality.

This becomes your baseline.


Phase 2: Main Game (15–25 Minutes)

Purpose: Train one identified skill.

Apply one shaping constraint.

Let it breathe.

Avoid constant novelty.

Let behavior reorganize.


Guardrail: Don’t Chase Activity

Fast transitions feel productive.

Frequent resets feel organized.

But learning deepens when pressure stays stable.

Fewer games.
Longer exposure.
Clear constraints.


Phase 3: Transfer Game (10–15 Minutes)

Purpose: Remove or reduce the scaffold.

Return toward normal play.

Observe:

Did learning survive?

If not, that’s information.

Not failure.


Phase 4: Reflection (3–5 Minutes)

Purpose: Make learning conscious without overloading it.

Short.
Focused.
Player-led when possible.

Then move on.


Anchor Games (Foundation, Not Decoration)

Define 3–5 stable environments you shape repeatedly.

Not reinvented daily.

Each should include:

Purpose
Reveals
Constraint Sliders
Failure Modes
Language to Avoid

Stability creates depth.


When You Stop Play

You intervene when:

- Safety is compromised
- Emotional dignity is at risk
- Representativeness collapses
- Confusion replaces challenge

Otherwise, let the system work.


REFLECTION

Design one full session using this architecture.

Run it once without adding extra drills.

Notice what changes.


Bridge Forward

You can now:

- Notice values
- See relationships
- Translate through constraints
- Architect sessions

The remaining variable is behavioral:

Your voice.
Your silence.
Your presence.

That’s next.
